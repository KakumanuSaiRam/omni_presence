NEET rewards two years of steady, NCERT-anchored work far more than last-minute heroics. Here is the plan we run with BiPC students at our Guntur centre.

## The foundation rule: NCERT is the exam

Over 80% of NEET Biology maps directly to NCERT lines. First year: read every NCERT Biology chapter three times — once for story, once for detail, once making margin notes. Physics and Chemistry need NCERT plus a standard problem book each.

## Year one (Inter 1st year)

- Finish 1st-year syllabus in sync with college — never let coaching outrun board classes.
- Weekly chapter tests; one revision day per week.
- Target: complete command of 1st-year Biology and Chemistry by summer.

## Year two (Inter 2nd year)

- Complete the syllabus by December.
- January onward: full-length NEET mocks weekly, then twice weekly.
- Every mock gets an error log: silly mistakes, concept gaps, time sinks. The log, not the mock, is where marks come from.

## Balancing IPE boards with NEET

The IPE weightage question resolves simply: the NEET syllabus **is** the Intermediate syllabus taught deeper. Students who dismiss board exams learn shallowly and it shows in NEET scores. Prepare once, properly, for both.

## Score-level honesty

A serious plan includes what happens at each outcome. 600+: government MBBS is realistic — counselling strategy matters enormously. 450–600: private MBBS, BDS, or a planned repeat year. Below 450: strong alternatives exist — B.Pharmacy via EAPCET, BSc Nursing, allied health sciences — and choosing one early beats a drifting repeat year.
