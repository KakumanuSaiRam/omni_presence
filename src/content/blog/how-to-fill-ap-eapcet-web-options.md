Every counselling season, students with excellent EAPCET ranks end up without seats — not because of their marks, but because of how they filled their web options. This guide, from the counselling desk at Siva Tuitions & Coachings, Guntur, covers the process end to end.

## Step 1: Know your real position

Your raw rank means little without context. Before touching the portal, work out your **category rank** (OC/BC/SC/ST), your **local area** (AU/SVU/non-local), and whether you qualify for special categories (NCC, sports, PH, CAP). Last year's cutoff ranks for each college-branch combination, filtered by your category, are the single most useful dataset in counselling.

## Step 2: Build the list on paper first

Never build your list live inside the portal. Prepare an ordered list — dream options at the top, realistic middle, safety at the bottom. Our desk typically prepares **100 to 150 options** per student. Filling only 10–20 options is the most common and most damaging mistake we see.

## Step 3: Order by your preference, not by cutoffs

The allotment algorithm walks your list top to bottom and gives you the first available seat. You lose nothing by putting ambitious options first. Order strictly by what you actually want: branch-first if you're set on CSE, college-first if you want a top campus with any branch.

## Step 4: Certificate verification, done early

Keep scanned and physical copies ready: SSC and Intermediate memos, EAPCET rank card and hall ticket, caste and income certificates (recent), study certificates for classes 6–12, and local-status proof. One expired income certificate can stall an allotment.

## Step 5: After allotment

Self-report within the deadline, decide on sliding rounds carefully, and never skip reporting to the college assuming a better round will come.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Filling too few options
- Copying a friend's list (different category = different reality)
- Ignoring pharmacy/agriculture options for BiPC students
- Missing self-reporting deadlines
- Treating spot admissions as a backup plan — by then the good seats are gone
