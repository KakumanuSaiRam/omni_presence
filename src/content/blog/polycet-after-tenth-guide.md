For thousands of tenth-class students in Andhra Pradesh, the polytechnic route is the smartest and most affordable path into engineering. Here is the complete picture.

## What is POLYCET?

**AP POLYCET** (Polytechnic Common Entrance Test) admits tenth-pass students into three-year diploma programmes at government and private polytechnics. The test covers Class 10 Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry — no new syllabus, just deeper command of what you already studied.

## Why families choose it

- **Cost**: Government polytechnic fees are a fraction of private Intermediate + B.Tech costs.
- **Time to earning**: A diploma holder can work at 18, or continue to B.Tech.
- **ECET lateral entry**: Diploma graduates enter B.Tech directly into the second year via AP ECET — often into better colleges than their tenth marks would have suggested.
- **For daughters**: AP runs dedicated **government polytechnics for women**, which many families specifically prefer. Guidance on these campuses and their cutoffs is a regular part of our counselling work.

## Who should choose polytechnic over Intermediate?

Choose POLYCET if the goal is engineering with limited budget, if the student learns better hands-on than through theory-heavy Intermediate, or if early employability matters. Choose Intermediate MPC if the student targets top-tier engineering entrances (JEE/EAPCET) and the family can support the longer runway. Two to three focused months are usually enough: strengthen Class 10 Maths (the largest section), drill Physics and Chemistry fundamentals, and practise previous papers under time.
