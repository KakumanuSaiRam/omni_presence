After a POLYCET rank, families ask for “the best polytechnic” as if it were one campus. **Government and private polytechnics** are different fee worlds, different lab realities, and different hostel stories. The right order on your web-options list is a preference plus a budget, not a brand slogan.

## Government polytechnics

Government (and government-aided, where applicable) campuses usually mean **lower fees** and a seat that many families specifically want. Competition for popular branches and towns is therefore real. AP also runs **government polytechnics for women** — a frequent request in our Guntur counselling room, including from parents who want a women-only campus rather than co-ed.

Inspect, do not assume: workshop condition, faculty presence, and whether the branch you want still runs with enough students to keep labs alive. A government name does not automatically mean a busy, well-equipped workshop in every trade.

## Private polytechnics

Private diplomas can be closer to home, have newer buildings, or offer a branch that did not open in the government list for your rank. They can also mean **higher fees** and variable training quality. Visit the workshop. Ask who teaches second-year core subjects. Ask last year’s ECET-attempt culture: does the campus treat lateral-entry B.Tech as a normal goal or as a rumour?

A private campus that is honest about fees and attendance is usable. A private campus that sells “placement guarantee” on a flex is not a counselling input.

## How to order options without caste-of-college snobbery

1. Write the **maximum fee** the family can pay if reimbursement is late.
2. List government campuses you would actually join (branch + town + hostel).
3. List private campuses that pass a visit or a trusted parent report.
4. Order by **your** preference: a slightly farther government mechanical seat vs a nearby private computer seat is a values choice. Put the true preference higher. The algorithm does not punish ambition.

Copying a friend’s POLYCET list fails for the same reason it fails in EAPCET: different rank, category and gender.

## Women’s campuses and co-ed: write the preference

If a government polytechnic for women is a non-negotiable, it belongs at the top of the band you would actually join — not as option 90 “in case.” If the student prefers co-ed closer to Guntur, say that too. Mixing an unspoken hostel veto with a long government list is how allotment day becomes a refusal. Phone counselling can rank women’s campuses by your POLYCET rank band without claiming we have visited every hostel in the state; we will still tell you to verify hostel rules yourself.

## ECET is part of the polytechnic choice

If B.Tech via **ECET** is the plan, ask how the diploma campus handles maths and core subjects in the final year. A cheap campus that does not teach is an expensive delay. Our graduate desk already sees diploma holders at ECET counselling; the students who arrive ready treated diploma years as engineering, not as “only practicals.”
