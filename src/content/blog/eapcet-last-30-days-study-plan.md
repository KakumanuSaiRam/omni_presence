Thirty days before EAPCET is not when you “start Chemistry.” It is when you **stop collecting new material** and start behaving like the exam: timed papers, ruthless review, sleep. This is the plan we use in short-term crash batches at Pattabhipuram. Adjust hours to your school timetable; do not adjust away the mocks.

## The only daily template that matters

- **One timed paper or two timed sections** (alternate days if stamina is low — but the clock stays)
- **Error log** — every wrong question tagged: concept / formula / silly / time
- **Repair block** — re-teach only the tags that keep repeating
- **IPE overlap** — board portions that are also entrance portions get one revision pass, not a second textbook

If you skip the error log, you are taking mocks for comfort. Comfort is not a rank.

## What to cut

Cut new “complete courses” on YouTube. Cut rewriting neat notes that you never look at. Cut studying a favourite chapter for the fifth time because it feels like progress. Keep a short list of **high-frequency** topics from official previous papers and your own mock weakness list — those two sources beat a random influencer’s “must-do 100 chapters.”

MPC students usually cannot hide from mathematics volume. BiPC students cannot hide from Biology memory plus NCERT-style wording where the paper demands it. Your personal weak list still wins over a generic slogan.

## Weekly checkpoints (four weeks)

**Week 1** — Full syllabus map on one sheet: done / shaky / untouched. Untouched chapters get a minimum viable pass or a conscious skip with a reason. Start grand tests.

**Week 2** — Two full papers. Repair the top three repeating error tags. Speed drills on calculation-heavy bits.

**Week 3** — Exam-week simulation: same start time as the real paper, same break rules. Revise formula sheets daily. Stop debating exam-centre travel; book it.

**Week 4** — Taper: shorter mixed sets, sleep, documents (hall ticket, ID). No all-nighters. A tired student invents silly errors that look like “I didn’t study Chemistry.”

## Health is a marking scheme

Skip meals and you will misread questions. Skip sleep and you will not finish the paper. Parents: this week is for quiet and food, not for new tuition shopping.

## Crash batch or solo — same rules

A crash batch only works if it is **test-first**. Ours is. If you stay solo, you still need a real clock and someone who will ask to see the error log. Bring that log to a doubt session; do not bring a stack of unread modules.
